# Scene composition and viewing-quality contract

## 1. Scene-value audit

Judge every source-scene element before carrying it into a variant:

- **NARRATIVE-FUNCTIONAL**: a character touches or uses it, it enables the action, it has a later payoff, it establishes a required location, or it communicates a story-established living condition.
- **AESTHETIC-ANCHOR**: it adds clear visual appeal, color separation, spatial shape or value perception without distracting from the action.
- **SOURCE-RESIDUE**: removing it leaves the story, location and composition equally clear.

Ask:

1. Would the shot become harder to understand if this element disappeared?
2. Does a character use it or will it matter later?
3. Is it required to recognize the location or social condition?
4. Does it provide a specific compositional benefit that another retained element does not already provide?

If all answers are no, remove or replace it. A clothesline, random vase, distant shed, fence, tool rack or source-specific decoration is not continuity merely because it appeared in the source frame.

## 2. Minimum narrative sufficiency

Build a scene from:

```text
required location + action-supporting space/prop + at most one nonfunctional aesthetic anchor
```

- Close and medium interaction shots need only the action-supporting surface plus one simple background identifier.
- Full-body and movement shots need a clear path or motion area plus one distant identifier.
- Spectacle and result shots may show more of the environment only when it communicates scale, wealth, transformation or payoff.
- Do not repeat character actions in `场景环境` when `描述` and `主体角色` already establish them.
- Describe the desired visible scene positively. Do not pad prompts with lists of things that must not appear.

Simple means controlled, not empty. Never add detail merely to create depth, premium feeling, continuity or difference.

## 3. Aesthetic-value rule

Each shot should have a deliberate source of visual appeal without becoming complex. Suitable anchors depend on the inherited style and story, for example:

- clean reflective water;
- a bright window-light area;
- one elegant tree or architectural silhouette;
- vivid fruit against controlled foliage;
- glass and warm gold reflections in a jewelry scene;
- a recognizable pool, villa or skyline in a wealth result.

Prefer clean surfaces, controlled color contrast, readable shapes and bright-but-natural materials. Avoid large muddy brown, dull gray, peeling, broken, dirty or cluttered areas unless the plot explicitly requires them. A rural road can be a clean light-colored path; rural does not automatically mean ugly or distressed.

Do not improve beauty by falsifying socioeconomic status. A modest home may be clean, bright and well-composed without becoming luxurious. Reserve luxury, skyline, villa or premium retail cues for story-established upgrades.

## 4. Subject dominance and framing refinement

Preserve the source shot size, angle, camera direction and framing purpose. `镜头` may be refined only to state:

- which face, interaction or functional prop holds focus;
- approximate subject prominence;
- required movement clearance;
- the appropriate depth of field.

Use scale as a flexible guardrail, not a mechanical quota:

- close or medium-close single subject: roughly 70–80% of frame height;
- medium single/dual interaction: roughly 60–70%;
- three-character, ground-prop or table-action shot: roughly 55–65%;
- walking, running or vehicle motion: roughly 50–60%, with free space in the movement direction.

When a percentage would create awkward cropping, state the visual hierarchy instead. Never force all scenery into the image; models often pull the camera back to fit every named element.

## 5. Depth-of-field selection

Do not apply one depth-of-field phrase to every shot.

- **Interaction, emotion, close prop operation, discovery**: shallow or moderately shallow depth of field; faces, contact point and functional prop remain sharp.
- **Walking, running, pushing, riding, room-wide collection or quantity spectacle**: medium depth of field; subject and usable motion space remain clear while the far background softens.
- **Wealth result, location upgrade, luxury reveal**: medium depth of field; characters and payoff props stay dominant, but the pool, villa, store or skyline remains recognizable.
- **Large transformation**: keep character and transformation effect sharp; soften nonessential surroundings without hiding scale.

Background softness should separate the subject, not erase the location. Keep explicit source focus behavior when it is essential to the shot.

## 6. Light and scene compatibility

Preserve established day/night, weather and light direction. Check that visible sources agree with the `光线` field: if the prompt says bedside lamp or pendant light is the source, retain one simple corresponding fixture when it is visible. Resolve contradictions with the smallest edit. Do not add LUTs, named cinematography styles or global color systems in this skill.

## 7. Continuity at the right level

Lock recurring structural attributes that viewers can notice:

- wall and floor family;
- bed, table, window and door design;
- recurring bedding and main furniture color;
- persistent functional props;
- location upgrade state.

Do not lock every lamp, flower, vase or distant tree. Micro-decoration is neither required continuity nor a useful way to differentiate versions.

## 8. Final audit

Before delivery, verify each shot:

1. Story and action remain instantly readable.
2. No source-residue element survived without a reason.
3. The scene is simple but not dull or aesthetically cheap.
4. The subject and functional prop dominate the frame.
5. Motion shots retain usable space.
6. Depth of field matches the shot function.
7. Recurring structures remain consistent.
8. Payoff scenes still show the environmental upgrade.
