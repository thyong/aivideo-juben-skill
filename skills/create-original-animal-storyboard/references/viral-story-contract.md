# Viral Story Contract

## Story card

Before storyboarding, state internally:

1. **Hook:** the abnormality, injustice, danger, embarrassment or striking contrast visible in one frame.
2. **Simple rule or goal:** what the viewer can understand without dialogue.
3. **Emotion:** sympathy, anger, curiosity, delight, pride, relief or another dominant response.
4. **Central promise:** the single question or result the viewer is waiting to see.
5. **Special-element role:** whether a required element such as gold is the catalyst, character, evidence, contested resource, tool, consequence or reward. Do not assign additional roles without narrative need.
6. **Causal chain:** how each event creates the next decision or consequence. Prefer `because -> therefore`; reject a sequence held together only by “and then.”
7. **Payoff:** the result that resolves the central promise, readable in one frame.

## Proven low-cost mechanisms

Use a small compatible set rather than stacking everything:

- visible abnormality or impossible object;
- clear family crisis, abandonment, humiliation or resource shortage;
- weak character taking visible responsibility;
- one-sentence magical or exchange rule;
- effort, care, training or making montage with visible progress;
- quantity, value or status change when it alters the story rather than merely enlarging the picture;
- kindness demonstrated before reward;
- strong before/after contrast;
- reunion, recovery, dignity restoration, wealth release or deserved punishment.

## Comprehension rules

- Prefer visual association, simple magic, goal causality or emotional causality over realistic explanation.
- A viewer should be able to retell the premise in one sentence after the first few shots.
- Skip travel, registration, sourcing, negotiation and other connectors unless they provide a distinct emotion, obstacle, visual spectacle or payoff.
- Skip tool-by-tool fabrication, training and repair detail unless that process is itself the changing spectacle. One process image plus one result image is the default for a single outcome.
- Preserve enough logic for the viewer to follow cause and effect. Do not add a second unexplained superpower to repair the first.
- Keep the number of essential roles small. Add a role only when it changes the conflict, progress or payoff.

## Naive visual causality

These stories need **instant comprehension**, not realistic completeness.

- Require enough cause for the viewer to understand the current desire, danger, trick or result.
- Permit one-step cartoon associations already familiar to the audience: gold is valuable, a treasure creates wealth, cash permits immediate enjoyment, a magical object produces its established result, and a greedy thief may suffer a direct comic reversal.
- Do not explain reward logistics after the audience already understands the result. `gold -> cash -> celebration` is complete; adding `buy equipment -> return to work -> demonstrate stable income` usually weakens the ending.
- Do not make characters spend rewards responsibly merely to repair every practical hardship shown in the setup. Visible wealth, luxury, relief, reunion, laughter or a strong comic punishment may be the final result.
- “Unthinking” does not mean random. Keep one established magical rule or simple emotional cause; avoid adding a second unrelated power, hidden condition or late moral.

## Story engine versus visual carrier

A proven visual element is evidence about presentation, not a mandatory plot structure.

- “Gold scenes perform well” means the story should use gold in visually meaningful beats. It does not require three rounds of gold growth, a gold shop, cash suitcases, luxury purchases or a gold mountain.
- A larger quantity, object or environment is new narrative information only when it changes what a character knows, chooses, risks, loses, gains or must do next.
- The story may keep gold visually present through a creature, location, prop, evidence or disputed object without increasing its amount.
- Select the story first, then place the visual element at the causal turning points. Do not build a story by filling a predetermined spectacle ladder.

## Originality gate

Originality means recombining validated audience mechanisms into a new causal chain, not inventing arbitrary complexity.

Reject a candidate when it keeps an existing sequence and changes only surface carriers, such as:

- strawberry to another fruit;
- gold to silver;
- one vehicle, animal species, costume color or shop type to another;
- identical humiliation, labor and revenge beats with renamed props.

A candidate is sufficiently new when it has:

- a distinct central problem or desire;
- a distinct visual engine or repeatable progress mechanism;
- a causal route not reducible to a known story with nouns replaced;
- a payoff earned by that route rather than attached at the end.

## Causal progression and payoff

- Progress within the established promise. A new beat should add a cause, decision, consequence, conflict, relationship change, resolved fact or earned result—not merely more scale.
- Quantity, value, status, emotion or danger may stay constant when the narrative is advancing through discovery, pursuit, care, deception, proof, reversal or justice.
- The payoff must not require dialogue or explanation. Prefer a single image that answers the story's central question: proof of innocence, completed rescue, reunion, recovered dignity, earned wealth or decisive justice.
- End shortly after the payoff. Do not spend several shots explaining consequences already visible.
- Stop at the highest-value audience image, not the most administratively complete outcome. Once the viewer has received the expected wealth, enjoyment, reversal, rescue or punishment, omit practical aftercare.
- Treat a familiar gold-shop exchange as an optional visual ritual, not an obligation. If included, do not automatically follow it with purchase, installation, work and proof-of-benefit scenes.

## Payoff runway and peak stop

The story may approach fulfillment through a short runway, but must end on the first image that completely satisfies the audience's central expectation.

- Use only the minimum 2–4 runway beats needed to increase certainty and anticipation, such as treasure reveal, disbelief, familiar appraisal and cash handoff.
- Runway beats answer partial questions: “what is inside?”, “is it real?”, “what will they receive?” The terminal image answers the central promise completely: “they are rich,” “the family is reunited,” “the villain is defeated,” or “the promised object is achieved.”
- Valid wealth sequence: treasure revealed -> gold-shop worker shocked -> cash handed over -> family celebrating among cash and gold **END**.
- Invalid post-peak sequence: family celebrating among cash and gold -> mansion -> sports car -> yacht. The first celebration already delivered wealth; later luxury images no longer carry an unanswered expectation and can reduce completion.
- A luxury object may be the terminal image only when the story established that exact object or status as the viewer's promise before the payoff. Do not fully deliver generic wealth first and then append it.
- Invalid responsible-closure sequence: treasure revealed -> buy a work vehicle -> learn to use it -> return to work -> prove stable income.
- Invalid repetition: the same gold pile, cheering pose or punishment shown from several angles.

## Rejection conditions

Redesign the story when:

- the hook arrives after several setup shots;
- the core rule needs multiple conditions, time cycles, moonlight, rituals or narration;
- most of the runtime is one real-time rescue, fight, chase or conversation;
- the requested runtime can be reached only by expanding one making, training, care, travel or repair block into several tool-level steps;
- the midpoint only maintains the same danger or emotion without progress;
- the midpoint substitutes larger scale for a new narrative consequence, or the ending repeats scale without adding a distinct payoff pleasure;
- the ending introduces friendship, sacrifice, romance, morality, revenge, transformation or punishment that was not an active promise before the midpoint;
- the payoff does not resolve the opening question, even if it is visually spectacular;
- the ending creates a new practical problem to solve after the core fantasy or conflict has already paid off;
- any shot remains after the first unmistakable full-delivery image, even if that shot is beautiful, luxurious or emotionally pleasant;
- the story depends on precise physical continuity that independent video generation cannot maintain.
