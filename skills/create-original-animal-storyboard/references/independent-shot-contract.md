# Independent Shot Contract

## Production model

One storyboard image produces one independent 6-second image-to-video clip. Production normally keeps about 3 seconds, sometimes 3–4 seconds, and hard-cuts to the next clip. The default one-minute deliverable therefore uses 20 valid shots rather than assuming every shot can be stretched toward 4 seconds. The editor must not need matched action, matched camera direction or a specific final frame.

Independence has two separate requirements:

1. **Production independence:** the clip does not require matched motion or geometry.
2. **Editorial independence:** before full delivery, the clip contributes a new narrative conclusion—decision, consequence, relationship change, conflict or resolved fact—or raises payoff certainty through reveal, disbelief or exchange. The first unmistakable fulfillment image is terminal; nothing follows it.

Passing production independence does not imply editorial independence. Six separately generatable clips of the same character building the same arm at the same workbench are still one overextended content block.

## Content blocks

Design 5–9 content blocks for a one-minute story. A content block is a stage defined by the viewer's current question, promise or expected result—not by the creator's list of physical steps. Typical blocks include:

- hook/discovery;
- conflict or shortage;
- decision;
- effort/care/build/test montage;
- escalation or setback;
- reveal/transformation;
- reward, justice or reunion.

A block can stay in one location. Scene change is not mandatory; information refresh is.

The payoff block must not grow into an aftercare block by default. Gold received, cash revealed, luxury enjoyed, family celebrating, villain defeated or victim rescued can already be the terminal state. Buying a practical object, showing it in use and proving that it improved life are separate subgoals and are invalid unless the opening explicitly made that exact result the audience promise.

The payoff block may use a **payoff runway** of normally 2–4 beats. Reveal, disbelief, appraisal and handoff may be separate when each preserves a clear unanswered question. Celebration, completed reunion, decisive punishment or the promised status object is not runway: it is the terminal full-delivery shot.

Use the **same-promise test**: if several shots can all be summarized as “the character continues making the same object,” “continues traveling to the same destination,” or “continues waiting for the same rescue,” they belong to one block even when the tools, poses or camera angles differ.

Before committing to a one-minute script, count the shots that survive this test. Under the current workflow, 20 valid shots are required by default. If fewer survive, reject or enrich the unresolved premise with new story stages, goals, relationships, obstacles, beneficiaries, locations or payoff anticipation before full delivery. Never manufacture capacity by subdividing a process, stretching assumed edit duration or appending images after the peak.

## Four hard tests per shot

### 1. Independent understanding

The shot alone makes its subject, action, emotion and core prop understandable.

### 2. Arbitrary trimming

Any stable 3–4 seconds selected from the generated 6 seconds can be used. The next shot must not depend on seeing the exact beginning or completion of this action.

### 3. Hard cut

The next shot works even if pose, screen direction, camera position and background geometry change. Preserve semantic state and identity, not cinematic match-on-action.

### 4. Simple motion

The shot supports one reliable motion such as walking, carrying, stirring, feeding, hammering, sewing, crying, pointing, selling, cheering or hugging.

### 5. Editorial value

Removing the shot would erase a distinct decision, consequence, relationship change, conflict, resolved fact or a necessary step in rising payoff certainty. A visual reveal, location stage, quantity increase or larger spectacle otherwise counts only when it changes what remains unresolved. After full delivery, no additional payoff pleasure justifies another shot.

## Valid repeated-shot functions

Multiple shots in one place are useful only when they create one of these:

- **Rule verification:** a second result makes the rule obvious.
- **Quantity/value change:** one becomes many or ordinary becomes precious and that change causes a new decision, consequence, conflict or payoff. Enlargement by itself is invalid.
- **Effort/care/build montage:** normally 1–2 consecutive shots for one result. A new tool alone is insufficient; additional shots require a different visible result, beneficiary, setting-stage or escalation that changes what the viewer is waiting for.
- **Chase/comedy escalation:** each shot adds a new location, obstacle, misunderstanding or joke.
- **Survival/task progress:** each shot solves a different need.
- **Reaction/relationship change:** power, trust, rejection or support visibly changes.
- **Reveal/reward loop:** every repetition reveals a genuinely new result.
- **Payoff runway:** before full delivery, 2–4 beats may increase certainty through reveal, disbelief, appraisal and exchange. End on the first celebration, completed reunion, decisive punishment or promised status image that fully resolves the expectation.

## Invalid motion splitting

Merge or skip shots that exist only to connect physical motion:

- approach object → reach for object → pick it up;
- prepare to throw → throw → object flies → receiver catches;
- push boat → board boat → begin rowing → row slightly closer;
- crack appears → crack grows → object almost breaks → object breaks;
- open door → cross threshold → walk down corridor → arrive.

Show the meaningful initial state and the next meaningful result. Let the viewer infer connectors.

Also merge same-result process lists such as:

- draw arm → saw metal → hammer plate → weld joint → connect wires → inspect arm;
- wash ingredient → cut ingredient → stir ingredient → plate ingredient when the meal itself is not the entertainment;
- exercise A → exercise B → exercise C → exercise D when all shots only mean “continues training” and no new challenge, social reaction or transformation appears.

For these, prefer one high-energy process shot followed by one completed-result or test shot.

## Content refresh audit

Across each 1–3 adjacent shots, refresh at least one meaningful dimension:

- dominant action;
- functional prop or tool;
- task or subgoal;
- role relationship or power state;
- emotion or decision;
- setting stage or time stage;
- visible progress;
- reveal, reward or consequence.

Changing only angle, pose, screen direction, distance, damage degree or background decoration does not count.

Changing only a hand tool while retaining the same character, location, goal and core object also does not count.

Changing only quantity, physical size, luxury level, crowd size, brightness or scene width also does not count unless the change alters the cause, goal, decision, conflict, relationship or consequence.

## Collapse and thumbnail tests

- **Collapse test:** replace a run with one process shot plus one result shot. If no essential story meaning is lost, the longer run fails.
- **Thumbnail-strip test:** inspect consecutive thumbnails without reading prompts. Three near-identical silhouettes dominated by the same character, place and object fail unless each contains a clearly different reward or consequence.
- **Removal test:** delete the middle shot. It fails when the story, emotion or unresolved payoff question remains unchanged. Any shot after full delivery fails automatically.
- **Narrative-conclusion test:** complete the sentence “because this shot happened, the character now knows/chooses/faces/gets/loses ___.” If the blank can only be filled with “more,” “bigger,” “another place” or “looks richer,” the shot fails unless that change directly drives the next event.
- **Peak-stop test:** identify the first thumbnail where the viewer can say “I already got the result I was waiting for.” That thumbnail must be last. Remove every later celebration, mansion, vehicle, feast, vacation, explanation or aftercare image; move runtime value earlier in the story instead.

## Prompt implications

- Describe one stable, readable visual state rather than a precise transition between two states.
- Avoid exact trajectory and handoff language when the next clip depends on it.
- Do not require “承接上一镜”, “动作无缝衔接” or exact left/right geography.
- Keep recurring assets consistent in identity, color, material and story state, while allowing flexible placement and camera geometry.
- Put critical characters, interaction and props in the same focal set. Background should identify place without competing with the subject.
